Hospital Uptime

Tiered solar, battery and generator plans that keep a hospital's life-critical loads on through 8 to 20 hours of daily grid outage.

The problem

  • Nigerian hospitals lose grid power 8 to 20 hours a day.
  • The default answer is one big diesel genset carrying every load, every outage hour.
  • That costs fuel every day, depends on fuel supply, and when it fails, oxygen, vaccine fridges and ICU monitors fail with it.
  • Administrators have no simple way to size a safer system or defend it to a lender.

The brief

"Design a scalable, low-maintenance energy system that guarantees uninterrupted 24-hour power for Nigerian hospitals experiencing 8–20 hours of daily grid outages."

The idea: power by tier, not by building

  • Tier 1, life-critical: oxygen, vaccine and blood fridges, ICU, theatre. Never shed; the generator is the last line.
  • Tier 2, essential: lighting, lab, water pump, records.
  • Tier 3, comfort: fans, AC. Shed first, never on diesel.
  • Solar carries the day, the battery carries the night, a small generator only backs tiers 1-2.
Hospital Uptime3 / 8

The product

  1. List the equipment and its tier.
  2. Set the grid: outage hours, start time, sun, diesel price.
  3. Auto-size solar, battery and generator.
  4. See every hour: which source carries each tier, what is shed.
  5. Stress-test 8-20 h outages, sunny and cloudy.
  6. See the money and print the plan.

How it works and how it is checked

  • Load per line = W × h / 24, grouped by tier.
  • Outage = one continuous block per day (worst case for storage).
  • Solar = half-sine 06:00-18:00 × 0.75 performance ratio.
  • Dispatch per tier: solar → battery → generator (tiers 1-2 only).
  • 3 simulated days, the 3rd reported.
  • 7 automated tests: auto-sized system never drops a life-critical load; comfort is shed first and never gets diesel; hybrid always burns less diesel than genset-only; stress range covered.
  • Prices are editable planning assumptions, not quotes. Surge, cabling and protection stay with a licensed installer.

Path to impact, 6 to 18 months

  • Pilot with one hospital: import real meter data instead of a typed equipment list.
  • Installer quotes plugged into the cost model; one plan, comparable bids.
  • Lender-ready export (monthly payment vs. diesel saved) for PAYG and asset finance.
  • Adapts anywhere: outage pattern, sun, tariffs and prices are inputs, so the same tool serves clinics in Africa, the Americas and Asia.
  • Free for hospitals; revenue from installers and lenders who receive qualified, pre-sized projects.
